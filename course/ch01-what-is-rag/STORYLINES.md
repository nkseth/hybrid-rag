# Chapter 1: What is RAG and why does it exist? (storylines)

The one idea: an AI doesn't know your documents, so RAG finds the right few pages first and hands them over as an open-book exam.
Spine: one question ("What's the refund policy for our Premium plan?") worked through from start to finish.

## A. Riya's First Day (recommended)
Device: a moment in someone's day. Form: one continuous take through one office.
A new employee, Riya, gets a customer question, guesses confidently and is wrong ("hallucination" is stamped across it). The scene rewinds: a runner fetches 3 pages from the filing cabinet, they land on her desk as [1] [2] [3], and her answer now cites them. The camera pulls back: the desk becomes the prompt and the cabinet becomes the database, showing the two phases (ingestion vs query). A 10,000-page pile buries her desk to answer "why not send everything?".
First 2s: Riya's desk, a chat bubble pops in with the refund question.
Beats: question → confident wrong guess → rewind → pages fetched → cited answer → R/A/G labels on the scene → why not everything → two phases → "RAG = an open-book exam".
Look: flat illustrated office, bold solid colours, Geist type (matches the app), one accent colour for the citations.
Sound: light office bed, paper whooshes, stamp thud on "hallucination".

## B. One Question's Journey
Device: the worked example. Form: object-to-object chain.
The question bubble becomes a search query, dives into a sea of chunks, the top 3 snap into the real prompt template, the prompt flows into the model, and the answer comes out with [1] [2]. It ends on the real Playground screen of this project.
First 2s: the question typed in the real Playground, then lifted off the screen.
Beats: question → search → chunks picked → prompt assembled → model → cited answer → the real app.
Look: the app's own dark UI, monospace for the prompt.
Sound: soft electronic bed, a click on each snap.

## C. Follow the Citation (bold)
Device: the result first. Form: an endless zoom backwards.
It opens on a finished answer. The camera dives into citation [2], which opens into the chunk, then the whole document, then the filing cabinet of all documents, then the database, so the whole pipeline is revealed in reverse. Then it zooms back out to ask: why not paste all of it in?
First 2s: an answer sentence with a glowing [2], and the camera is already moving into it.
Beats: answer → [2] → chunk → document → cabinet → database → back out → why not everything → one-line idea.
Look: one infinite canvas, white, ink-black type, a single accent.
Sound: a rising zoom tone at each level.
